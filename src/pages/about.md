TITANIUM 3DP is the titanium additive manufacturing engineering and RFQ site of Suzhou Como Precision Materials Co., Ltd. It is built for custom titanium components rather than consumer 3D printing or general metal-printing news.

The site is for engineers and purchasing teams deciding whether a titanium part is worth quoting through additive manufacturing. Typical inquiries involve Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-6Al-4V ELI, CP titanium, lightweight structures, internal channels, part consolidation, HIP, machining, inspection evidence, and traceability requirements.

Our commercial objective is specific: turn a part idea, drawing, or CAD package into a reviewable manufacturing scope and a comparable delivered-part quotation. The scope includes application fit, alloy and process route, finishing, inspection, records, lead time, and acceptance responsibility—not only the printing operation.

## What We Do

We review drawings, CAD files, quantities, alloy targets, lead-time requirements, operating conditions, post-processing needs, inspection scope, and documentation expectations for titanium AM projects. The starting routes are [material selection](/materials/), [application fit](/applications/), [manufacturing capabilities](/capabilities/), and the [RFQ input checklist](/rfq/).

If the drawing and requirements are clear, we can quote from the information provided. If important details are missing, we ask focused clarification questions before quoting.

## What We Review

Common review points include material grade, LPBF or EBM process fit, DfAM limits, support removal access, distortion risk, machining allowance, surface finish, stress relief, HIP, CMM, CT, coupon requirements, density evidence, COA/COC, and traceability level.

The review depth depends on the part. A simple prototype may only need CAD, quantity, and material target. A function-critical part may need operating conditions, acceptance criteria, and inspection requirements before a useful quote can be prepared.

## How We Publish Engineering Guidance

The [engineering guide library](/case-studies/) is organized around decisions that affect manufacturability, delivered cost, lead time, acceptance evidence, or supplier comparison. We do not treat an application name by itself as proof that additive manufacturing is the right route.

Our editorial rules are:

- Preserve the difference between a published standard, a supplier capability, a design assumption, and a part-specific acceptance requirement.
- Link to primary standards bodies, regulators, research institutes, or original manufacturer disclosures when a technical or current-industry claim depends on them.
- State when a value is illustrative, route-dependent, or must be confirmed on the drawing and purchase order.
- Avoid invented customers, unnamed project results, fabricated test data, and claims of certification or approval that are not documented.
- Update an existing URL when the search intent already exists instead of publishing near-duplicate keyword pages.

Publication dates and material updates are shown on guide pages. If a cited standard is revised, a source changes, or a technical statement needs correction, contact us with the page URL and supporting evidence. We will review the source and correct the page without hiding the decision boundary.

## Capability and Acceptance Boundaries

Titanium additive manufacturing is not automatically the best route for every titanium part.

Simple geometry, tight all-over tolerances without machining access, high-volume cost sensitivity, or high-risk parts without an evidence budget may be better made by machining, casting, or forging. Application references on this site are screening guidance, not proof that a particular part is qualified, certified, or approved.

Machine size, validated material condition, post-processing source, test method, accreditation, documentation, and final acceptance capability can vary by supplier and route. These items must be confirmed in the quotation and controlled purchase requirements. For regulated or safety-critical hardware, the design authority and purchaser retain responsibility for the applicable specification, qualification plan, and acceptance decision.
