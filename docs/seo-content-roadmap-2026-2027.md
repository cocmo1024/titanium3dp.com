# TITANIUM 3DP SEO Content Roadmap, 2026-2027

## Objective

Build a durable organic-acquisition system for engineers and purchasing teams who need titanium additive manufacturing parts. The site must win searches by resolving a real sourcing or engineering decision, then move qualified visitors toward an RFQ with clear scope, evidence, and acceptance requirements.

The target is not the largest possible page count. The target is broader qualified-query coverage, stronger topical relationships, and more RFQs from pages that have a distinct reason to exist.

## Positioning and Business Boundary

TITANIUM 3DP is an English-language B2B engineering and lead-generation site for custom titanium additive manufacturing components. Its commercial scope is:

- Ti-6Al-4V / Grade 5 / TC4, Ti-6Al-4V ELI / Grade 23, and CP titanium routes.
- LPBF and EBM fit, with other titanium manufacturing routes discussed only as comparison or sourcing alternatives unless capability is confirmed.
- DfAM, support and powder removal, heat treatment, HIP, machining, surface condition, inspection, traceability, documentation, and delivered-part quotation scope.
- Application screening across aerospace, medical, robotics, energy, corrosion, thermal, flow, and critical-minerals hardware.

The conversion event is a technically reviewable inquiry: CAD or STEP, drawing or requirement notes, material, quantity, operating environment, critical features, post-processing, inspection, records, and required date.

## July 2026 Inventory Baseline

Before this roadmap wave, the repository contained 39 engineering posts. The inventory showed:

- 24 titles built around the repeated `Titanium 3D Printing for X` application pattern.
- Strong coverage breadth but weak application discoverability: the application hub exposed only a small subset of the existing domain guides.
- Thin but heavily linked foundation articles covering material selection, DfAM, post-processing, qualification, and process selection.
- Repeated language in corrosion/process-flow, flight/lightweight, robotics, and thermal/flow clusters.
- 166 tag labels before this wave, including 126 single-use tags. The current taxonomy is over-fragmented, and tag pages are not a primary acquisition strategy.
- All posts stored under the historical `Case Studies` category even though most pages are engineering guides, not documented customer cases.

This wave retains established URLs and canonicals, rewrites six foundation URLs, adds two non-overlapping evidence topics, exposes the existing application library by decision cluster, and changes the visible library label to `Engineering Guides` without changing the `/case-studies/` route.

## Topic Architecture

### 1. Commercial and Material Intent

Purpose: help a buyer define the material and delivered scope before requesting prices.

Current assets:

- Materials hub.
- TC4 / Ti-6Al-4V / Grade 5 specification path.
- Ti-6Al-4V ELI / Grade 23 specification path.
- Grade 2 / TA2 / CP titanium procurement path.
- Capabilities and RFQ pages.

Rule: add another alloy or service landing page only when it maps to an actual quotable route and differs materially in chemistry, condition, standard, acceptance, or buyer task.

### 2. Manufacturing Decisions

Purpose: capture durable searches that determine feasibility, cost, lead time, and supplier comparison.

Core cluster:

- Material selection.
- LPBF vs EBM vs deposition-route selection.
- DfAM, supports, orientation, walls, and powder escape.
- Post-processing, HIP, machining, and surface condition.
- Cost drivers, lead time, AM versus CNC, tolerances, internal channels, fatigue, and no-go screening.

Rule: prefer decision matrices, failure modes, acceptance ownership, and RFQ inputs over generic process descriptions.

### 3. Qualification and Evidence

Purpose: turn vague requests for “certification” or “quality” into a route-specific evidence plan.

Core cluster:

- Part qualification, CMM, CT, NDT, coupons, traceability, and release records.
- Powder reuse, oxygen control, lot genealogy, sampling, and conformance.
- In-situ monitoring and melt-pool data: what it can detect, what it cannot prove, and how it can support—but not replace—qualification.
- Fatigue-sensitive surface and HIP decisions.

Rule: distinguish standards for terminology, process control, test methods, imperfection classification, and acceptance. A sensor signal, density result, or HIP cycle is not automatic proof of part acceptance.

### 4. Application Engineering

Purpose: meet domain-specific searches while avoiding doorway pages.

The application hub groups existing guides into:

- Flight, space, and lightweight mobility.
- Medical and patient-specific structures.
- Robotics and high-technology equipment.
- Energy and critical infrastructure.
- Corrosion, water, thermal, and flow hardware.
- Critical-minerals and process-development hardware.

Rule: an application page survives only if it has a distinct operating environment, failure mode, standards boundary, geometry problem, or procurement task. An industry name plus reused titanium benefits is not enough.

## Publishing Gate for Every New URL

A proposed page must pass all gates before drafting:

1. **Distinct intent:** the query cannot be satisfied better by expanding an existing URL.
2. **Commercial fit:** the reader can plausibly progress to a titanium component RFQ.
3. **Unique decision:** the page resolves a different material, process, acceptance, failure, or purchasing decision.
4. **Evidence:** technical and current claims can be supported by primary standards, regulators, research institutes, or original manufacturer disclosures.
5. **Internal-link home:** at least one hub and two directly related pages have a natural reason to link to it.
6. **Conversion path:** the page can state the exact files, conditions, tests, and records needed for a useful quote.
7. **Maintenance owner:** there is a defined trigger for review, such as a standards revision, capability change, Search Console evidence, or material industry development.

If a proposal fails gates 1-3, merge it into an existing page. If it fails gates 4-7, hold it rather than publishing a thin placeholder.

## Update Cadence

### Monthly

- Export Google Search Console page and query data using a fixed comparison window.
- Review pages with rising impressions but weak CTR for intent/title mismatch.
- Review pages with clicks but weak RFQ engagement for scope, internal-link, or conversion gaps.
- Check index coverage, canonical host consolidation, sitemap processing, broken internal links, and structured-data validity.
- Refresh one to three existing URLs before approving new URLs.

### Quarterly

- Compare query overlap inside each cluster and identify consolidation candidates.
- Recheck external standards and source links used by high-impression pages.
- Review material, process, inspection, and application hubs for missing child links.
- Audit title patterns, excerpts, single-use tags, and claims that may have become stale.
- Record RFQ source pages, qualified inquiry rate, and inquiry themes without exposing customer-confidential data.

### Semiannually

- Re-evaluate business capability and remove or qualify any scope the supplier network cannot substantiate.
- Decide whether thin application pages should be rewritten, consolidated with redirects, or excluded from indexing using fresh page/query evidence.
- Rebalance the roadmap around actual non-brand demand and conversion value, not predicted keyword volume alone.

## Internal-Link Rules

- Hubs link to every validated child page in the cluster.
- Foundation guides link contextually to the material, process, qualification, and RFQ pages needed for the next decision.
- Application guides link to cross-industry method pages for fatigue, channels, tolerance, powder control, qualification, or post-processing instead of repeating those sections.
- New pages must earn links from existing relevant bodies, not only from a universal footer.
- Anchor text should describe the next decision; avoid repetitive exact-match keyword anchors.
- Preserve established URLs, canonicals, breadcrumb structure, and the RFQ path during rewrites.

## Measurement

Track outcomes by cluster and landing page:

- Indexed canonical pages and sitemap health.
- Non-brand impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position from Search Console.
- Queries and pages that expand beyond brand/material-name searches into engineering and procurement decisions.
- Visits from guides to materials, capabilities, applications, and RFQ pages.
- Email and WhatsApp contact actions where measurable.
- RFQs attributed to an organic landing page.
- Qualified inquiry rate: inquiries containing enough part, material, quantity, environment, and acceptance information to begin engineering review.

Do not treat published page count, word count, or an unverified search-volume estimate as a success metric.

## Failure Conditions

Pause incremental publishing if any of these occur:

- New pages repeat the same benefits, tables, RFQ blocks, or sources with only the application name changed.
- Index coverage grows while non-brand impressions and qualified inquiries remain flat across two quarterly reviews.
- Content makes unverified capability, certification, customer, test, or performance claims.
- A page cannot name its unique decision, evidence boundary, and conversion path in one sentence each.
- Maintenance demand grows faster than the ability to recheck standards, sources, links, and commercial scope.

The corrective action is inventory reduction, consolidation, and stronger hubs—not faster page production.
